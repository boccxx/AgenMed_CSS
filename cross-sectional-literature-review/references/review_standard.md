# Review standard

Use this file as the single detailed reference for the literature-review skill.
The goal is an auditable evidence package that can inform study design,
analysis, and manuscript claims.

## 1. Intake

Resolve these fields before searching:

- population and setting;
- exposure, comparator, and outcomes;
- study design and question type;
- review goal and delivery level;
- date, language, and source limits;
- the downstream decision the review must support.

Record unknowns as open questions. Never fill missing study facts with a
plausible assumption.

## 2. Evidence workflow

1. Define eligibility criteria and the search question.
2. Record each source, exact query, date, limits, hit count, retained count,
   and execution status.
3. Deduplicate records and retain an exclusion reason for every screened record.
4. Appraise study design, measurement, bias, directness, and applicability.
5. Extract study, outcome, effect, confounder, citation, and evidence-to-decision
   rows only when the source supports them.
6. Verify citation identity before using a paper to support prose.
7. Synthesize by evidence role and topic, not by a list of paper summaries.

## 3. Evidence boundaries

- Cross-sectional evidence supports prevalence or association statements. It
  does not establish temporality or causal effects.
- Do not convert OR to PR or RR, or treat statistical significance as evidence
  of causal importance.
- Do not select confounders by univariate significance or citation frequency.
- Distinguish measured evidence, author interpretation, and the review's
  inference.
- A missing full text, inaccessible source, or unresolved citation identity is
  a declared limitation, not a completed reading.

## 4. Synthesis

Every substantive claim in the narrative should map to a verified citation and,
when it reports an effect, to an extracted evidence row. Write each paragraph
around a decision:

1. state the evidence-supported pattern;
2. compare consistency and disagreement;
3. explain measurement, population, or design reasons for variation;
4. state the remaining uncertainty;
5. give the exact implication for the downstream study.

Separate descriptive burden, exposure-outcome association, measurement,
confounding, mechanism, subgroup, and contradiction evidence. Do not combine
these roles into one undifferentiated score.

## 5. Delivery levels

- `brief`: search record, screening status, core evidence rows, and a short
  briefing;
- `decision_grade`: complete registries, evidence sufficiency judgment, and
  protocol bridge;
- `full_package`: decision-grade outputs plus citation verification and export.

Downgrade explicitly when evidence access, search completeness, full-text
coverage, or directness cannot support the requested level. The package status
must say what is complete, what is blocked, and what decision remains unsafe.

## 6. Handoff

Keep these objects distinct:

- retrieval and search log;
- screening and exclusion decisions;
- evidence registries;
- internal review briefing;
- reader-facing synthesis;
- protocol inputs and proposal bridge.

The bridge may inform objectives, variables, measurement, confounding, and
uncertainty. It must not silently freeze sampling, final sample size, ethics,
or causal claims.
